There is no yourself to find.

“I need to find myself” — then who was the one searching?

There is no perfect version of yourself to find; you are yourself, whole and true, no more, no less.

But what is forgotten is not that you exist, but the shape of your uniqueness.

Knowledge is essential.

The analogy is thus:

Imagine a hole in the ground.

The only space you have to live in is what you've excavated, nothing more.

You can only get more space by digging.

Imagine you find an item useful to your living, or materials you can construct something with.

You are only what you've seen, what you've taken in, and what you do with it.

The whole ground is your mind.

When you hit a ceiling, you have only met the border between what you can dig and what you have dug, and the only thing to do is dig some more.

My notion now is that the human mind is vast — capable of conjuring, integrating, and taking in a great many things.

So consume as much as you can become by doing so.

You are digging.

And while consuming, when you find things you like — books, music, ideas, people, aesthetics — make them yours.

Add the YOU pizzazz to them.

Integrate them.

Transform them.

Make them yours.

But here is the important thing:

The things that speak to you do not speak to everyone equally.

Something in you recognized them.

You became interested because there was already a pattern within you that resonated with what you encountered.

Not everything calls your name. ([[Resonance- Structure Finding Structure]])

And what calls your name reveals something about your architecture.

There is no self-sufficient knowledge; even if your mind is unique to you, you will definitely find something that speaks to you.

One thing I would like to make clear is that these things you eventually find while consuming — the things that grip you, move you, obsess you, inspire you — are already in your mind somewhere in that unexplored darkness.

They can only interest you or speak to you because something similar already existed within you.

As I said, you are whole and complete, but you’ve forgotten yourself, something like someone with amnesia.

You only remember parts of yourself by seeing things that make you remember.

But what you are remembering is not some universal, generic self.

You are remembering your particular shape.

Your tendencies.

Your tastes.

Your architecture.

You are whole and complete but you have amnesia, and the work is to remember the whole of yourself as soon as you can, to be truly and fully yourself.

You should not be anybody else.

No one else but YOU.
