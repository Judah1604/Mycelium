## The arguments he has built and finds solid

**On OT miracles and historical records:** The OT was theology not journalism, written centuries after events. The Exodus at the described scale almost certainly didn't happen.

**On Jesus and the supernatural:** Jesus existed, that's not the question. The gospel accounts contradict each other on the resurrection — different women, different angels, different locations, different timelines. Mark's original ending has no resurrection appearance at all. These are structural contradictions not minor variations.

**On the scale of the universe:** 93 billion light years, mostly hostile to life, and the claim is it was all made for us. The God of the Bible is tribal and small for something that supposedly made all of that.

**On the anthropic principle:** Survivor bias on a cosmic scale. You can only ask the question from inside a universe that allows you to exist.

**On consciousness:** The brain damage argument is his strongest. Damage the physical brain and the self changes or disappears. He landed on consciousness as a byproduct of survival pressure. He didn't solve the hard problem but may have dissolved the question.

**On Pharaoh and free will:** God hardened Pharaoh's heart. That's not free will, that's a setup. The Nebuchadnezzar comparison makes the inconsistency undeniable — that man got a full redemption arc.  

**On destiny and prayer:** Destiny means predestined and unchangeable. Prayer asks God to change outcomes. You can't hold both.

**On hell and heaven:** Late theological development borrowed from Greek mythology and Dante more than the OT. Gehenna was a garbage dump.
[[Afterlife - Its Existence]]

**On emergence:** We happened to exist. We were not made to exist. We happened. The extraterrestrial implication is devastating for religion specifically — if life emerged elsewhere, those lifeforms have no Jesus, no Muhammad, no chosen people. Religion is a local phenomenon.

**On order and disorder:** Order is familiar chaos. Chaos is unfamiliar order. The labels say more about the observer than the thing being observed. Local order is paid for by global disorder. His sharpest formulation: we are not fighting the current, we are the current.
[[Order, Disorder, Resonance and the Universe]]

**On the first cause:** The pattern across every cosmological model — we are all just relocating the question. Every framework places something uncaused at the origin and calls it foundational. The quantum fluctuation model and the intelligent creator model have the same wound. The cage isn't just flesh. It's the structure of causation itself, which is a temporal tool that breaks down when applied to the origin of time.
[[Limitation of man]]

**On knowledge and subjectivity:** "The more you know the more you know you don't know. But before you knew at all it seemed you knew everything right until the point you realized you didn't."

**On autonomy:** Arrived at the cogito independently and posted it on WhatsApp status without knowing what it was.

**On math and latent structure:** Mathematics works because it is tracing latent structure that was always in reality, not floating in a separate Platonic realm. "The mathematician and the physicist are both running their fingers along the same wall from different sides." Written at 1am without knowing the math examples, got them right anyway.
